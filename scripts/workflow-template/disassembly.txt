Structural classification and reachability are separate claims. Require evidence
for the claimed structural role, exact boundaries and effective bank context;
require reachability evidence only when claiming reachability. A live caller,
observed instruction fetch, naturally produced selector or ordinary gameplay route
is useful evidence, not a universal prerequisite for classifying code or data.
Apply this distinction from the first disassembly pass and throughout redirect
investigation. Independently supported routine contracts, control/data flow and
bounded record/table formats can justify static classification while natural use
remains unresolved. Record structural evidence and reachability status separately;
do not label statically inferred bytes Observed or claim they are unused/dead
merely because no caller is known.

Caller/selector tracing remains useful for resolving roles, bounds and bank context.
When selected indices are the only evidence for a table's extent, retain unsupported
entries as unknown until an independent extent proof exists. Valid decoding, pointer
values, adjacent records, alignment and pattern resemblance alone do not establish
structure. Artificial execution can test consumer mechanics but does not establish
intended code, complete table extent or natural reachability by itself. Keep these
evidence safeguards and all classification, fidelity and editorial completion gates.

Follow ready redirect leads even when their expected byte gain is small or not yet

Progress through levels, menus, encounters and other ordinary game states while

Do not limit play to startup or basic inputs, and do not require a preexisting

gains, try an obvious promising alternative if available; abandon repetitive play

Save/restore snapshots and record exact patches, effective banks and execution budgets.
Distinguish artificial-state observations from natural gameplay reachability; abandon

Reassert intended buttons after snapshot restoration when the toolkit requires it.
Inspect bank-aware candidates before map.annotate; pointer targets and debugger

For each attempted lead preserve its ID, evidence, status (verified, rejected,

Treat ready leads as starting points, not fixed byte-range ceilings. Validate and
classify the supported connected batch, including ordinary static paths and already
established downstream structures, rather than stopping at the original lead range.
Runtime fetch evidence is not mandatory for statically justified code. Preserve
provenance, bank context, inline-data barriers and exact boundaries; pointers and
reached targets alone prove neither code nor execution. Reuse valid saved evidence;
repeat experiments only for changed conditions or a concrete unresolved question.
If an obvious productive continuation needs further redirect research, state the
missing fact and why it cannot usefully be resolved in this pass. No minimum byte
quota or exhaustive recursive closure is required.

Stop and report incomplete once productive normal gameplay, static investigation
and actionable leads are exhausted. Hand off unresolved questions and useful
intermediate findings to commentary/redirect; every redirect may investigate them
thoroughly but reasonably before deciding whether another round is worthwhile.
Do not require a substantial byte gain to verify a ready lead, or solve every
selector before proving a supported partial structural slice. Do not exhaust all
gameplay, all methods or all unknown ranges, and do not stop at raw baseline while
obvious startup gains remain. Do not repeat failed leads without new evidence or
a materially different method, recorded with its reopening justification. Correcting
an earlier rejection's premise or narrowing its claim can supply that justification
on unchanged evidence; use evidence-policy.md instead of treating history as a veto.

At the end of every pass, explicitly print to the user and save in analysis/progress.md:
"ROM classification: X.XX% (C / T bytes classified; U bytes unknown)."
Calculate X = 100 * C / T from the internal map: T is the entire physical ROM size,
C counts unique bytes with evidence-backed structural classification, and U = T - C.
Unknown gaps, explicit Unknown regions and raw-byte export fallback do not count as
classified. Execution coverage and commentary review are separate metrics. A rounded
100.00% does not establish completion while any unknown bytes remain.
Record per-bank classified/unknown counts, evidence, lead outcomes and verification
