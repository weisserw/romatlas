# Round001 interpretation limits

The source explains the mechanics of every classified instruction fragment. This is a readability result over the current map, not a claim that the ROM is classified completely or that every game meaning is known. Unknown intervals remain binary includes. Labels for tables in those intervals are equates derived from their consumers; they do not identify full table boundaries or classify bytes.

- Physical 32..120: bank 0 supplies indexed pointer dispatch, an eight-byte record address calculation and an indirect byte reader. The complete record format and data consumers beyond the observed scene wrappers remain unresolved.
- Physical 12304..12638: actor frame pointers and dimensions drive descending OAM construction. The missing row-reset branch, other frame variants and the exact offscreen-sentinel conventions need more classification. The rendering budget and rotation are established; game-specific actor names are not.
- Physical 16416..20848: scene rendering expands map cells into paired tile columns and packed attribute quadrants. Scene stream/page/junction/route names reflect observed mechanical use, not a complete stage graph. Unclassified special-ID and alternate-orientation paths prevent a full scene format specification. The startup contract needed to reach later routes remains the earlier L001 question.
- Physical 32800..61108: menu, inventory, map and dialogue fragments are identified by observed entry behavior and their consumers. Text timing and command comparisons are explicit; handlers for $e6/$e7/$fc/$ff/$fd remain unclassified. Their complete meaning and the ordinary progression input sequence remain L002. Status values 0..3, optional inventory widgets and many tile/effect IDs retain mechanical names because item/score/currency identities are not established.
- Physical 65568..74889: actor stream decoding and eight logical audio voices are explained. Voice offsets 0..20 are named only where their mechanical role is used. Four upper voices mask lower voices in register output; this suggests priority overlays, but the complete music/effect assignment is unresolved. Command/envelope/note tables are consumer aliases without classified extents. Physical 74688 BIT skips an alternate LDA at 74689; this source preserves the classified BIT view and does not invent overlapping instruction classification.
- Physical 81952..113646: actor behavior variants retain numbered mechanical names. Quantized movement, contact samples, frame changes, animation delays and state dispatch are documented; exact enemy/item identities are unresolved. Actor auxiliary fields are unions: saved-coordinate storage and counters/flags depend on behavior. Player trajectory/contact/camera logic is clear where classified, but many special-mode paths remain gaps. `player_vertical_state`, `player_motion_variant`, `player_optional_state` and related names intentionally describe limited observed roles rather than inventing game conditions.
- Physical 114960..131036: reset is only partially classified. Fixed-bank wrappers, MMC1 bank stack, NMI/OAM/controller/PPU services are explained, with effective physical bank identities separated from serial register values. MMC1 addresses $9fff/$bfff/$c000 select control/CHR0/CHR1 respectively; split helpers change CHR1. No full reset or hardware compatibility claim follows.
- The seven uniform-$ff classified data spans reuse their original boundary/neighbor/fetch review. No specific padding, allocator or table purpose was established. CHR is explained as sixteen-byte two-plane tiles; individual art, tile ownership and animation sets remain unresolved.

Useful bounded follow-up remains in the existing L001/L002/L003 leads: recover scene initialization from a genuine transition, inspect text-command consumers and static dispatch closure under valid bank contracts. These questions do not defer any currently classified source commentary. The known source-readability backlog is empty; future classification or contradictory evidence may require targeted review.
